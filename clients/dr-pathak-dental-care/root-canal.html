Treatments / Root canal treatment

Save the tooth. Lose the pain.

There’s no need to be worried. A root canal removes the infected nerve from inside the tooth, cleans and seals it, and stops the pain at its source. With good anaesthesia, most people say it feels like a long filling.

Is it for you?

You might need this if…

  • Lingering pain with hot or cold
  • Throbbing pain that wakes you at night
  • Swelling or a small boil on the gum near a tooth
  • A tooth that has turned dark after an injury

Step by step

What actually happens in a root canal.

Five steps, usually over one or two visits. Tap through them.

Decay reaches the nerve inside the tooth. It swells, can’t escape, and hurts. Infection can spread to the bone at the root tip.

The tooth is fully numbed. A small opening is made in the top to reach the canals.

Fine instruments remove the infected nerve and disinfect each canal, end to end.

The clean canals are filled with a rubber-like material and sealed so bacteria can’t return.

A crown protects the treated tooth so you can chew on it normally for years.

What to expect

After your root canal

  1. 1

    On the day

    Wait for the numbness to wear off before eating, and chew on the other side.

  2. 2

    First few days

    Mild tenderness is normal. Ordinary painkillers help.

  3. 3

    The crown

    Back teeth are best protected with a crown, because a treated tooth can become brittle.

  4. 4

    Long term

    Brush, floss and keep your check-ups. A well-treated tooth can last a lifetime.

FAQ

Questions people ask

Is a root canal painful?

The tooth is numbed with local anaesthesia. It’s the infection that hurts; the treatment relieves it.

Isn’t it easier to pull the tooth out?

Removal is quicker today, but a gap brings its own problems: neighbouring teeth drift, and replacing the tooth later costs more. Saving your own tooth is usually the better choice.

Do I need a crown afterwards?

For back teeth, usually yes, to prevent cracks. Front teeth can sometimes manage with a filling.

Can it be done in one visit?

Often, yes, if the infection isn’t severe. Some cases need a dressing and a second visit.
