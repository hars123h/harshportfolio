01

Why is it important to see a dentist?

Most dental problems are quiet at first. A small cavity, early gum disease or a cracked filling rarely hurts until it is bigger, deeper and more expensive to fix.

A dentist can spot these changes years before you notice them, often with nothing more than a look, a gentle probe and an X-ray. Treating a cavity early means a filling instead of a root canal; treating gum disease early can mean keeping teeth that would otherwise loosen.

Your mouth is also connected to the rest of you. Gum disease is linked with diabetes and heart health, and dentists are often the first to notice signs of grinding, acid reflux or mouth ulcers that need attention.

In short

  • See a dentist every six months, even if nothing hurts
  • Come sooner for bleeding gums, sensitivity or a broken tooth
  • Bring a list of your medicines to every visit

02

Common dental procedures: you and your dentist

Knowing what a treatment involves makes it far less worrying. Here are the procedures people ask us about most, in plain words.

A filling repairs a cavity with tooth-coloured material, usually in one visit. A root canal cleans infection from inside a tooth and seals it, so the tooth can stay. A crown is a cap that protects a weak tooth. A bridge or an implant replaces a missing tooth, and dentures replace several or all.

Whatever the treatment, you should always know the options, the number of visits and the cost before it starts. Good dentistry is a conversation, so ask anything.

In short

  • Ask what happens if you wait
  • Ask for the options, not just one plan
  • Ask how to look after the work afterwards

03

Common teeth problems, and where aligners fit in

The problems we see most are cavities, sensitivity, gum disease, worn or chipped teeth, and teeth that are crowded or out of line.

Crowding isn’t only about looks. Overlapping teeth trap food, are harder to brush and floss, and can wear unevenly. Straightening them can make the whole mouth easier to keep healthy.

Clear aligners are a series of thin, removable trays that move teeth a little at a time. They are nearly invisible and come out to eat and brush, which suits many adults. Ceramic braces are the alternative when a fixed, full-time option works better.

In short

  • Sensitivity to cold can be an early warning sign; get it checked
  • Bleeding gums are not normal, even if they don’t hurt
  • Aligners only work if worn 20–22 hours a day

04

Dental check-up: why is it important?

A check-up is short, painless and the best value appointment in dentistry. It usually includes an examination of every tooth, the gums, tongue and cheeks, a check of your bite and any old fillings or crowns, and X-rays when needed.

Most check-ups end with a scale and polish. Even the best brushers build up tartar in places a toothbrush can’t reach, and only a professional clean removes it.

If you wear dentures, check-ups still matter. Gums change shape over time, and a loose denture can rub sores that should be seen early.

In short

  • Every six months for most adults
  • Children from their first tooth, or by age one
  • Denture wearers at least once a year
