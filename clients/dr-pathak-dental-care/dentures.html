Treatments / Dentures

Dentures that stay put, and look like you.

Dentures are a core part of a prosthodontist’s training: how they grip, how they bite, and how they support your lips and cheeks. Whether you need a few teeth or a full set, we make them to fit your mouth and your face.

Is it for you?

You might need this if…

  • Several or all of your teeth are missing
  • Your old denture is loose, cracked or makes your face look sunken
  • You want to avoid surgery, or want implants to hold a denture firmly
  • A tooth must come out and you don’t want to go a single day without teeth

Four kinds of denture

Which one is right for you?

They differ mainly in how firmly they hold. More grip usually means more comfort when you eat, and a little more time and cost.

Complete denture

A full set of teeth on a gum-coloured base, held by suction and the shape of your gums.

Grip while eating
Best for
All teeth missing in a jaw. A reliable, affordable start.
Care
Remove nightly; brush with a denture brush.

BPS denture

A premium full denture made with a precise, step-by-step system and high-grade teeth for a closer fit, firmer bite and more natural look.

Grip while eating
Best for
People who want the best possible full denture without surgery.
Care
Remove nightly; reline as gums change.

Cast partial

A thin, strong metal framework with teeth attached that clasps onto your remaining teeth. Lighter and less bulky than a plastic plate.

Grip while eating
Best for
Several teeth missing, especially at the back where a bridge can’t reach.
Care
Remove to clean; brush the clasps and your teeth.

Implant-retained

A denture that snaps onto 2–4 implants, so it doesn’t slip when you eat or talk, yet still comes out to clean.

Grip while eating
Best for
Lower dentures that float; anyone tired of denture adhesive.
Care
Unclip to clean; brush around the implant heads.

What to expect

How it goes, visit by visit

  1. 1

    First impressions

    Accurate moulds of your gums, taken with trays made for your mouth.

  2. 2

    Bite and face

    We record how your jaws meet and how much lip support you need, so your face looks natural, not sunken or puffed.

  3. 3

    Try-in

    You see and try the teeth set in wax before the denture is finished. Shape and shade can still change now.

  4. 4

    Fit and follow-up

    The finished denture is fitted, then checked over a couple of short visits to ease any sore spots.

FAQ

Questions people ask

How long does it take to get used to new dentures?

Usually a few weeks. Speech settles within days; chewing takes a little longer. Start with soft food cut into small pieces.

Should I sleep with my dentures in?

Usually not. Taking them out at night lets your gums rest. Keep them in water or a cleaning solution.

My denture is loose. Do I need a new one?

Not always. Gums shrink over time, and a reline can restore the fit. If the teeth are worn down, a new denture or implant support may be better.

Can I get teeth on the day a tooth is removed?

Yes. An immediate denture can be fitted the same day so you are never without teeth. It is relined once the gums have healed.
