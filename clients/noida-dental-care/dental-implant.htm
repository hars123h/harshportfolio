Software-guided implants

Implants planned on a 3D scan and placed through a guide, often without gum cutting.

Freehand vs software-guided

Three reasons it’s better

Minimally invasive

No need to cut the gums, raise flaps and stitch. A small opening is enough: less bleeding, faster healing, less discomfort.

Precise

An intraoral and CBCT scan builds a 3D model of your jaw. The surgery is planned on software around bone density and nerves, and an acrylic guide drills at the exact position.

Predictable

Because everything is planned beforehand, the crown lines up naturally with your surrounding teeth.

How it works

  1. 3D scanIntraoral and CBCT scans of your jaw.
  2. Plan on softwareExact depth, angle and position, decided around your anatomy.
  3. Custom guideA physical acrylic guide is made from the plan.
  4. PlacementThe implant goes in through the guide via a small opening.
  5. Your crownA crown aligned with your smile.

If the bone is weak

Sometimes the scan shows poor bone quality or density, more common in diabetics and smokers. Then an additional procedure such as bone augmentation may be needed, and PRF from your own blood can help healing.

A tooth is priceless

No amount of money can replace a natural tooth, so Dr. Anjana fights to save it first. When extraction is unavoidable, an implant is the next best option: it preserves the bone and gives you a tooth you can chew with comfortably.

Questions

How long does healing take?

The bone heals around the implant over 3 to 8 months, and the final crown is placed after about 4 to 6 months.

Does it hurt?

The area is numbed, and guided surgery through a small opening usually means less discomfort afterwards. Some pain and swelling for 7–10 days is normal.
